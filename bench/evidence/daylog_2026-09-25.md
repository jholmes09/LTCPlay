## Incident: the Pico froze solid, 2026-09-25 about 10:12

**What Jeff saw:** the screen froze, Ctrl+Alt+Delete did nothing, the Start button did nothing. Jeff restarted it by hand; it was back at 10:27.

**What was running:** MadMapper Demo (new project, Conductor paused on External Sync, no timecode arriving), the Claude app, two Windows Settings windows, TeamViewer and the Stream Deck app in the background. Nothing of mine was running: the last timecode packet went out at 10:09:39, my last screenshot was at 10:09:52 and my last file write at 10:10:15. Windows' own "last known alive" mark is 10:11:38, so it froze between then and about a minute later, with the PC otherwise idle.

**What Windows recorded:** only "the system has rebooted without cleanly shutting down" (Kernel-Power 41, bug check code 0, no long power-button press recorded) and "the previous shutdown at 10:11:38 was unexpected" (6008). No blue screen, no crash dump, no graphics-driver reset, no hardware error, no app crash or hang, and nothing at all in the logs between 10:04 and the freeze. That pattern (everything stops, nothing gets written, Ctrl+Alt+Delete dead) is a whole-machine hang below the level of any one program: the graphics/display system, a driver, or the hardware itself. A program like MadMapper, ltcplay or the Claude app can freeze its own window, but it cannot stop Ctrl+Alt+Delete.

**This PC's history** (the System log goes back to August 2024; this unit has clearly been used elsewhere before): 14 unexpected shutdowns since January 2025, including two within five minutes on 2025-12-29 and two on 2026-03-11. Some of those may be power being pulled rather than freezes; the log cannot tell the difference. Never a blue screen. One Intel graphics driver reset (2026-01-01). Three corrected-hardware notices (WHEA, informational) in October and November 2025. Over its life it has had at least 15 different monitors attached, plus TeamViewer, Parsec and two more virtual-monitor tools.

**Suspects, most likely first:**

1. **The virtual-monitor drivers.** There were four add-on "fake monitor" drivers (MikeTheTech Virtual Display Driver, Amyuni USB Mobile Monitor from 2021, TeamViewer's virtual monitor, Parsec's virtual display). One of them failed to load at every boot today, and one was live. Drivers of this kind (Windows "indirect display" drivers) plug straight into the display system that also draws the Ctrl+Alt+Delete screen, and a stuck one can hang the whole desktop in exactly this way. Nothing in the show needs any of them. **Removed on Jeff's instruction at 10:36** (log: `remove_virtual_monitors_2026-09-25_1036.log`): all four devices and their driver packages are gone; the only display driver left is Intel's.
2. **The Intel graphics driver under MadMapper.** MadMapper keeps the Intel graphics busy drawing even when paused. Driver 32.0.101.7080 (November 2025). It reset itself once before, on 2026-01-01.
3. **The hardware or firmware.** A small, passively cooled box; BIOS 64U005A4 from June 2024; Windows reports at every boot that firmware is holding one core at reduced speed. Can't be ruled out until the soak.
4. **My screen control.** The screenshots and simulated clicks I used to set up MadMapper were the most unusual thing the display system did today. They had stopped about two minutes before the freeze and they run as an ordinary program, so they are an unlikely cause, but not a proven innocent one.

**How likely again:** unknown until tested, honestly. The 14 earlier unexpected shutdowns say this unit has done this before. Removing the virtual monitors removes the likeliest cause, but only a long run under show load proves it. The soak test is that proof.

**What now, without replacing the PC:**

- Done: virtual monitors removed. A restart finishes it.
- Done: a flight recorder (`flight_recorder.ps1`, writing `flight_<date>.csv` every 5 seconds: processor, memory, graphics load, top programs). If it freezes again, the last lines show what the PC was doing. It changes nothing on the PC. It stops when the PC restarts; I start it again each session.
- Next: rerun the tests, then the multi-hour soak with MadMapper playing video, ltcplay sending, and the recorder on. If it runs clean for hours, suspect 1 was very likely it.
- If it freezes again: Jeff's decisions, because each changes Windows: (a) switch on a "crash on demand" key so the next freeze leaves a crash dump that names the driver, (b) a clean reinstall of the Intel graphics driver, (c) ask VIOSO for a BIOS update and this unit's history, (d) a clean Windows install on the Pico, which a show PC with this much history arguably deserves anyway.
- Two things spotted on the way, for Jeff: **TeamViewer is installed and running** (remote access into the show PC; keep it for remote support, or remove it before the show), and **the Elgato Stream Deck app is running**. The safety program will need the Stream Deck to itself (handoff section 15), so the Elgato app must not be running on show nights. **Removed on Jeff's instruction at about 10:45**: Elgato Stream Deck 7.0.3 and its Elgato Volume Controller helper, uninstalled cleanly (MSI exit 0, log `remove_streamdeck_2026-09-25.log`); no Elgato programs, folders or devices left.

## Incident 2: the Pico crawled, 2026-09-25 from about 12:00, and heat is now the main suspect

**What happened:** from about 11:58 (right after BEYOND was opened), anything that started a program, compiled a helper, captured the screen, listed windows or asked Windows about storage took from 30 seconds to over 10 minutes, or never returned. The flight recorder, which writes every 5 seconds, stalled for 20 to 57 seconds at a time. Processor use stayed low (8 to 20%), memory flat (8.2 to 8.5 GB free), MadMapper kept playing and stayed at 2.2 GB. At 12:40 or so Jeff clicked the Claude app with the mouse and the whole desktop, video playback included, froze and then came back. BEYOND never showed a window; it was force-closed at 12:14 (it took a few seconds to die) and that did not cure the slowness. MadMapper was closed at about 12:59; the stalls continued with the PC idle (recorder gaps of 30 to 46 s at 13:00 to 13:03 with processor at 2 to 7%).

**Evidence:**
- One disk sample at about 12:18 (5 x 2 s): disk 144% busy, **1.4 seconds per read**, queue 1.4, almost no bytes read (204 B/s). A healthy SSD answers in well under a millisecond. The drive-health and disk-error queries (Get-PhysicalDisk, System log) hung and were abandoned, so this rests on that one sample.
- Jeff, about 12:45: the large heatsink on top of the Pico is **too hot to keep a hand on for more than a second or two** (roughly 60 C or more on the outside of a fanless box).
- The processor held about 260% of base clock under the stand-in load and about 127% under the 1080p load; it returned to about 305 to 315% once idle, so the processor itself was not stuck throttled at idle.
- Defender (MsMpEng) was idle during the disk sample.

**Reading:** a fanless box that has been under steady load for two hours, with a heatsink too hot to touch, plus storage requests taking over a second, is the textbook picture of an **SSD throttling itself on heat**. When Windows cannot read its own disk, everything that needs a file waits; if the drive stalls long enough, the whole desktop freezes, Ctrl+Alt+Delete included. That fits this morning's freeze better than the display drivers did. **Heat is now suspect 1**, above the virtual-monitor drivers. Not proven yet: the proof is whether the stalls vanish when the box is cooled, and come back when it is hot again.

**The SSD's own report, 13:10** (Windows' built-in drive counters, read as administrator on Jeff's yes; file `ssd_health_1307.txt`), after about 10 minutes nearly idle:

| | |
|---|---|
| Drive | Kingston OM8SEP41024Q-A0, 1 TB NVMe, firmware SBI00111 |
| Windows health flag | Healthy, wear 0 |
| Temperature now | **60 C** (after 10 minutes idle, with the heatsink still hot) |
| Temperature max reported | 83 C |
| **Longest read** | **20,473 ms: a single read took 20 seconds** |
| **Longest write** | **19,101 ms** |
| Read / write error counts | not reported by this drive |

A 20-second read is the whole story of both incidents: while the drive holds a read that long, anything that needs a file waits, and a long enough wait freezes the desktop and Ctrl+Alt+Delete with it. The drive is not reporting itself worn out or failing, so the question is why it stalls. Two candidates, one test each:
1. **Heat.** 60 C at idle is hot for an NVMe drive; under two hours of load inside a fanless box it very likely reached the range where these drives throttle hard. Test: cool the box and see whether the stalls stop and the temperature falls.
2. **The drive's power saving or firmware.** NVMe drives drop into low-power states between requests; some models and firmware take seconds to wake, and Kingston's OEM drives have a history of this. Test (Jeff's call, Windows settings): Power Options, PCI Express, Link State Power Management **Off**, and ask VIOSO or Kingston whether firmware SBI00111 has an update.

**Second reading, 13:16** (`ssd_health_1316.txt`), PC still nearly idle: temperature **65 C (up 5 C in six minutes at idle)**, longest read and write unchanged (no new stall worse than the record), longest flush 28,495 ms. A drive warming at idle means the box is not shedding heat, or the drive is not resting; either way it points at heat and airflow first.

**13:19, one change:** Jeff lifted the Pico about an inch off the wooden desk so air can move underneath (the processor heatsink on top was already clear; the SSD is probably on the underside of the board with no heatsink). Nothing else changed.

**Result at 13:35** (`ssd_health_1335.txt`): SSD **61 C, down from 65 C** at 13:16; stall records unchanged (no new long stall). Flight recorder, PC idle throughout:

| Window | Samples | Longest gap between 5-second samples | Gaps over 15 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13:00 to 13:19, flat on the wooden desk | 118 | 63 s | 17 |
| 13:19 to 13:35, lifted an inch | 159 | 7 s | **0** |

The stalls stopped the moment air could reach the underside, and the drive started cooling. Strong evidence that **the SSD overheating is the cause** of today's freeze and crawl. Still to prove: that it stays clean under show load with the airflow in place (a load run while watching the drive temperature), and what the rack enclosure needs.

**Found on the way: Windows gives admin rights here without asking.** `ConsentPromptBehaviorAdmin = 0` ("Elevate without prompting"), `PromptOnSecureDesktop = 0`. The PowerShell windows Jeff was told to click Yes on never asked; every admin action today (virtual-monitor removal, Stream Deck uninstall, SSD readings) had Jeff's yes in chat first, but Windows itself would have let any program elevate silently. For a show PC on the rack network, Jeff should put the User Account Control slider back to the default before the show (his call; not changed).

**Power supply:** less likely. A failing or undersized supply usually shows as sudden shut-offs or reboots, not a slow crawl. Not ruled out.

**Next, and what it means for the show:** Jeff is putting a fan on the heatsink. If that cures it: the rack enclosure needs airflow across the Pico (even in November the enclosure traps heat), probably a small fan on the heatsink, and the video format should be chosen to keep the load light (the panel-sized stand-ins used 14% processor against 78% for 1080p H.264). An SSD temperature reading needs an admin tool (for example CrystalDiskInfo), which is Jeff's call to install.
